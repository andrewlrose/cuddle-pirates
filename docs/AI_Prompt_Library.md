# PART I: BEAR SUIT MAN

---

## I-A. Creative Lineage

BSM's suit design lives in a specific tradition of the junkyard superhero — one person, available parts,
genuine engineering conviction applied to a catastrophically misapplied premise.

**Primary real-world ancestor:** Troy Hurtubise's Ursus Mark VI bear-proof suit (documented in
*Project Grizzly*, 1996). Composite materials, chain mail, bold painted geometric markings, removable
helmet. The suit *looks* competent. The premise is not undermined by the construction.

**Primary comic lineage:** "Junkman" — Mirage Studios, Jim Lawson character design (1990).
Junkyard superhero with labeled components: pail helmet, hubcap throwing stars, yield sign chest
shield, barbed wire wrapping, vacuum cleaner club, lunchbox boots. BSM is a direct descendant.
Reference: `reference/JunkmanLawson.jpg`, `reference/JunkmanDooney.jpg`

**Closest existing AI draft:** `reference/REF/doctor_furious_fcf0e34b.png` — correct human scale,
junkyard armor vocabulary, goggles, wheels and gauges. Best on-model AI reference currently available.

## I-C. The Suit — Steampunk Junkyard Bear-Repelling Apparatus

The suit is built in a garage by a man who took his own paranoid engineering seriously enough to
actually finish it. Every component has a reason. The reasons are all sound. The premise is
catastrophically misapplied.

**Design principle:** The Hurt Locker bomb disposal suit crossed with a Victorian boiler room and
a junkyard superhero origin story, assembled from a Harbor Freight catalog and two weekends of
junkyard runs. The Junkman, but for bears.

---

### SUIT — Full Component Description (medium and close-up shots)

```
homemade steampunk junkyard bear-repelling suit:

HELMET — an oversized vintage leather football helmet reinforced with riveted brass strips,
a custom cage facemask of hand-bent copper pipe and brass rod welded into an irregular grid —
bars slightly uneven, garage-welded by one man — brass-rimmed goggles pushed up on the forehead,
a small rear-view mirror mounted on a copper pipe arm extending from the back of the helmet
(to monitor bear approach vectors from behind without turning),
two small pressure gauges on the crown on copper pipe stems, one hand-labeled in marker,
his face fully readable through the copper cage — expression visible, unobscured

CHEST — salvaged car hood section hammered roughly flat as breastplate,
a large central gauge face hand-repainted to read BEAR THREAT on a scale from 0 to EXTREME,
two flanking plumbing gauges, pressure release valves on each shoulder pointing outward,
copper tubing crossing the chest, red flannel visible through structural gaps,
everything bolted not welded — the whole apparatus shifts slightly when he moves,
leather straps and military surplus buckles

ARMS — shovel-head shoulder pauldrons riveted to leather straps,
dryer hose wrapped in leather strips at the upper arm,
diamond-tread non-skid aluminum plate scales at the elbows (overlapping fish-scale segments),
dog trainer jute bite sleeves covering the forearms — thick woven jute rated for large breeds,
a spring-loaded boxing glove mechanism on each forearm, stowed flush when not deployed,
rubber tire tread banded with copper strips at the wrist transition,
copper chain tassels at the elbow joints — they clink with every movement

BACK — a chrome garden pressure sprayer tank mounted between the shoulder blades on a welded frame,
hand-pump handle accessible over the right shoulder,
copper tube running to the chest apparatus and left forearm nozzle,
REAR GUARD: a fart/flame-thrower deterrent unit mounted at the lower back,
the mechanism is never fully explained — it is there, it has a trigger, that is sufficient,
back section partially finished: hose clamps, zip ties, one bungee cord section

LEGS — corrugated metal thigh guards strapped over heavy denim,
diamond-tread non-skid plate knee guards matching the elbow pieces,
barbed wire coiled around the shin sections over PVC pipe backing,
tall armored boots extending up past the knees — laced and strapped,
rubber tire tread at the boot ankles for material continuity
```

## I-H. Composing a BSM Prompt

**Template — Documentary Storyboard (Mode 1):**
```
[SHOT FRAMING] shot, [ENVIRONMENT BLOCK],
[BSM ACTION — suit block scaled to distance:
  wide = silhouette desc / medium = component desc / CU = full desc],
[BEAR if present],
[COLOR RULE if both in frame],
static frame, impassive, film photography style, Kodak grain
```

**Template — Character Design (Mode 2):**
```
[SUIT BLOCK at component level],
[EXPRESSION/POSE — face readable through copper cage, rear-view mirror visible],
[THREAT or HUGGABLE modifier],
[MODE 2 STYLE BLOCK],
--ar 2:3 (character sheet) or --ar 16:9 (scene)
```

**Example — Mode 1, medium shot:**
```
medium shot, supermarket cereal aisle, fluorescent institutional overhead lighting,
cold green-white color cast, flat institutional environment

a man in a heavy homemade steampunk junkyard bear-repelling suit stands at the
cereal shelves — oversized leather football helmet with copper pipe cage facemask
and rear-view mirror arm, bulky brass-fitted chest with BEAR THREAT gauge,
jute bite-sleeve forearms, shovel-head shoulder pauldrons, chrome pressure tank
on his back, the suit is warm brass and copper amber — the only warm object
in a cold fluorescent world

he holds a shopping list close to the copper cage, reading through it requires effort

static frame, impassive, film photography style, Kodak grain
```
